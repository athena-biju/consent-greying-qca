4.2 Codebook: Definitions, Examples, and Decision Rules

Code Label Category Definition Example DecisionRule
1.1 Grade and assessment power Professional / Academic Context Grades, exams or evaluation used or felt as leverage or control. Jokes about 'do they accept blowjobs because we’re failing' hinting at grades as bargaining chip. Use when assessment (marks, passing, failing) is explicitly tied to power or favours.
1.2 Reference and supervision dependency Professional / Academic Context Reliance on a mentor/supervisor for references or supervision that constrains ability to say no. Older men framed as 'the only ones who can really help you' with career/studies. Anchor is references/supervision, not grades alone.
1.3 Career vulnerability Professional / Academic Context Fear that speaking up or refusing will harm future jobs, funding or opportunities. Worry that institutions protect reputation or funding rather than victims. Use when silence/compliance is linked to fear for future opportunities.
1.4 Institutional cover-up / reputation management Professional / Academic Context Institutional actions that minimise, hide, or rebrand abuse to protect image. School builds new campus, has her sign papers not to speak about what happened. Use when institution actively manages image; prefer 5.1 when emphasis is emotional betrayal.
2.1 Paternalism ('fatherly', 'older and wiser') Age and Symbolic Authority Older men presented as caring/protective or father-like while abusing power. Told he is 'the only person who cares', 'good guy', despite exploitation. Age explicitly tied to caring/guiding role.
2.2 Experience and expertise aura Age and Symbolic Authority Age/status framed as superior knowledge or wisdom that legitimises control. Seeing life experience as what makes manipulation effective. Code when age = 'they know better/what they’re doing'.
2.3 Older men as uniquely 'safe' or 'stable' Age and Symbolic Authority Older men depicted as more stable/serious/safe partners despite power imbalance. Attraction framed as finally finding 'a man' or safety. Age itself is the reason for perceived safety/desirability.
3.1 Prior mental health and trauma Vulnerability and Susceptibility Existing mental illness or CSA/trauma increasing susceptibility to grooming and self-blame. Being back in therapy; Zoloft/melatonin; long CSA history. Use when mental health history is linked to being targeted or staying.
3.2 Social isolation / lack of support Vulnerability and Susceptibility Family, peers or professionals fail to protect, believe, or support. Brother reads diary and asks if she made it up; institutions tell her to keep quiet. Focus is on absence of support or disbelief.
3.3 Pre-existing dependency patterns Vulnerability and Susceptibility Depending on unsafe adults/institutions for safety, housing, grades or needs. Being 'chained' via threats to friends; punishment instead of care. Use when dependence itself (housing, protection, daily life) is leverage.
4.1 Denial as coping Meaning-Making of Consent Not naming abuse as abuse to survive or function. 'If I hadn’t been told this is abuse, I wouldn’t have hung on.' In-the-moment non-recognition; for later re-interpretation see 4.3.
4.2 Romanticising exploitation / media scripts Meaning-Making of Consent Using romantic/cinematic narratives from films, novels or porn to reframe abuse. Pretty Woman, Lolita, teacher–student movies, 'sexy' domination. Explicit reference to media or consciously 'making it into a love story'.
4.3 Retrospective non-consent Meaning-Making of Consent What felt 'chosen' or 'normal' then is now clearly seen as non-consensual. 'This was not my choice' / 'I saw no way out.' Contrast between past agency and present view of coercion.
4.4 Self-blame and responsibility Meaning-Making of Consent Participants blame themselves for not leaving, not speaking up, or 'allowing' abuse. 'How the hell was I that stupid?'; 'Maybe I could have done more.' Focus is on their own supposed failure, not institutions.
5.1 Institutional betrayal and abandonment Institutional Responses and Ethics Emotional sense of being abandoned, silenced or disbelieved by protective institutions. 'They antagonised and distanced from me and my advocacy.' Affective betrayal foregrounded; 1.4 when emphasis is on image management.
5.2 Framing as private issue vs policy vs reputation Institutional Responses and Ethics How institutions label these relationships: private romance, minor misconduct, or policy/PR issue. Cases treated more as PR crises than harm. Focus is categories/discourse used by the institution.
5.3 Uncertainty about procedures / ethics channels Institutional Responses and Ethics Confusion about where to report, what processes exist, or consequences. Not knowing which ethics channels are binding; realising later what she signed. Emphasis is on not knowing procedures; gagging/NDAs can also be 1.4/5.1.
